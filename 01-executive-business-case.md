# 01 · Executive Business Case

> **Executive summary (read this box).**
> Cisco + Splunk is now the most complete portfolio in the market — secure networking, full-stack observability, and security operations under one roof. But we still sell it architecture-by-architecture, so customers buy point products and we leave the largest, stickiest, outcome-led deals on the table. We propose funding a **Field CTO seller group**: a small cadre of senior, business-fluent technologists who lead **cross-architecture (X-Arch)** opportunities in four focused verticals. They sell business outcomes to the C-suite, then orchestrate the BU specialists to land multi-architecture solutions.
> **The ask:** fund a **12-person pilot for 4 quarters** (8 Field CTOs + 4 support/leadership) at an illustrative fully-loaded cost of **~$5.4M/yr**. **Target return:** influence **$180M+** of pipeline and **$60M+** of incremental/accelerated bookings in year one, with **Splunk cross-attach** as the leading indicator. **Payback:** under 12 months at the modeled attach uplift. Full model in [`06-financial-model.md`](06-financial-model.md).

---

## 1. The problem

Cisco sells one of the broadest technology portfolios in the world, and the Splunk acquisition made it broader and more strategic. Yet the field motion has not caught up:

- **We sell in silos.** Networking, Security, Observability/Splunk, and Data Center each have their own specialists, quotas, and conversations. The customer experiences four Cisco conversations, not one.
- **We talk speeds-and-feeds, not outcomes.** The people who can map a customer's *business* problem (resilience, risk, cost-to-serve, safety, compliance) to a *multi-architecture* Cisco + Splunk answer are scarce and unscaled.
- **Splunk cross-sell is under-attached.** The single biggest post-acquisition synergy — attaching Splunk observability and security to the massive Cisco networking/security install base (and vice versa) — depends on someone who can credibly connect both worlds in front of a CIO/CISO.
- **We lose the "architecture of the deal."** In large accounts, whoever frames the reference architecture wins the multi-year budget. Today competitors and SIs often own that frame; we arrive to bid on components.

Net effect: smaller deal sizes, slower cross-sell, weaker C-suite relationships, and margin left with integrators.

## 2. Why now (the post-Splunk window)

The window to establish an outcome-led, cross-architecture motion is open **now** and closing:

- **Portfolio completeness.** For the first time, one vendor can credibly own the full stack from the industrial edge and campus network, through the WAN and firewall, up into observability and the SOC. That is a genuinely differentiated story — but only if someone tells it end-to-end.
- **Integration momentum.** Cisco XDR + Splunk ES, ThousandEyes + AppDynamics + Splunk, and Cyber Vision + Splunk OT are maturing into real joint solutions. A senior field motion accelerates adoption while the integrations are fresh and newsworthy.
- **Budget consolidation.** Customers are actively consolidating vendors for cost and risk. The consolidation conversation is a C-suite, cross-architecture conversation — exactly the Field CTO's home turf.
- **AI infrastructure pull.** AI-ready data center builds are pulling networking, security, and observability decisions forward together, creating natural X-Arch entry points.

## 3. The solution — a Field CTO seller group

A **Field CTO seller** is a senior, quota-influencing technologist who:

1. **Owns the business conversation** with the CIO/CISO/COO — framed in the customer's language (risk, resilience, cost, safety, growth), not product SKUs.
2. **Architects across BUs** — designs the reference architecture that spans Secure Networking, Observability/Data, and SecOps (plus AI-ready infra), then pulls in BU specialists to detail and close each component.
3. **Leads with vertical credibility** — carries deep pattern knowledge for their industry (e.g., NIS2 for transportation, DORA for financial services), so the customer treats them as a peer, not a vendor.
4. **Orchestrates, doesn't hoard** — acts as the connective tissue between account teams, BU sellers/SEs, Splunk specialists, CX, and partners. Their success metric is the *total* multi-architecture outcome, not a single-product quota.

This is deliberately **not** another product-overlay SE team. It is a thin layer of senior "deal architects / trusted-advisor sellers" whose leverage is the whole portfolio. Role definition, leveling, and org design are in [`03-role-requirements.md`](03-role-requirements.md).

### The X-Arch thesis in one line

> When one credible person frames the customer's outcome across three architectures, average deal size, cross-attach, and win rate all rise — and the customer relationship moves from vendor to strategic partner.

## 4. Focus (so the pilot can prove the model)

To make the pilot measurable, we concentrate on the architectures and verticals with the highest cross-attach and clearest boardroom narrative.

**Architectures ("3 + 1"):** Secure Networking · Full-Stack Observability & Data · Resilience & Security Operations · (+1) AI-Ready Infrastructure.

**Verticals (4):** Transportation & Logistics · Financial Services · Healthcare · Public Sector / Critical Infrastructure.

Rationale and the detailed plays are in [`02-market-strategy.md`](02-market-strategy.md) and [`04-vertical-solution-map.md`](04-vertical-solution-map.md).

## 7. Why this beats the alternatives

| Alternative | Why it falls short |
|-------------|--------------------|
| **Do nothing** | Splunk synergy under-realized; competitors/SIs keep owning the reference architecture; deals stay small and siloed. |
| **Add more product-overlay SEs** | Deepens silos; nobody owns the cross-architecture, outcome-led C-suite conversation. |
| **Rely on partners/SIs to integrate** | Cedes the strategic frame and margin; weakens Cisco's direct C-suite relationship. |
| **Expand generalist account teams' remit** | Account teams lack the deep multi-architecture + vertical technical credibility to lead at CxO level. |

The Field CTO group is the **thin, senior layer** that makes the *existing* specialist teams more productive by giving deals a cross-architecture frame and a C-suite sponsor.

## 8. Risks (summary — full treatment in [`07-roadmap-risks.md`](07-roadmap-risks.md))

- **Talent scarcity** — the profile (senior, business-fluent, multi-architecture) is rare → mitigated by internal identification + selective external hires + a strong enablement ramp.
- **Quota/comp friction** — overlay influence vs. BU quotas can create conflict → mitigated by an influence-credit model and a shared-success comp design.
- **"Another overlay team" perception** — → mitigated by keeping it thin, senior, outcome-measured, and orchestration-first.
- **Attribution** — proving influence → mitigated by control-group measurement and a deal-desk-tracked influence ledger.
